Rationale

Why these projects?

Economics and financial markets increasingly operate in an environment characterised by information abundance. Economic data, institutional publications, geopolitical developments and regulatory updates are produced continuously across hundreds of fragmented sources.

Collecting that material is slow, repetitive and adds little analytical value in itself. Interpreting it is where the work actually lies.

Can AI and automation help professionals spend less time collecting information and more time analysing it?

Each project below is an attempt to answer that question in a specific domain, under one constraint: AI is not the source of facts. Data is retrieved from official sources, the statistics are computed and validated deterministically, and a language model is used only afterwards, as a constrained language layer. The economic reasoning and the final judgement remain human and traceable to primary sources.

Projects

Three domains

One project per information domain. Project 01 is complete and documented as a case study; the remaining two are scoped and not yet built.

Project 01

Completed

Macro Intelligence Brief

Economic data → Structured insight

A reproducible reading of the United States macroeconomic picture. Official data is collected, the conventional transformations are computed and validated deterministically, and only then does a language model draft the prose — checked back against the figures before anything is published.

Project 02

Planned

Regulatory Intelligence Monitor

Regulatory publications → Relevant developments

Filtering the continuous stream of supervisory and regulatory output down to the developments that actually bear on a given mandate, with every item traceable to the originating document.

Project 03

Planned

Geopolitical Risk Monitor

Events → Economic transmission

Tracking geopolitical developments and mapping their plausible transmission channels into prices, trade flows and policy, separating the observed event from the inferred economic consequence.

About

Economics student exploring the intersection of economics, financial markets and emerging technologies. This portfolio is a set of working experiments rather than finished products: each one documents its method, its assumptions and its limitations alongside its output.

These projects are research exercises. Nothing here constitutes investment advice, no output is a forecast, and nothing should be relied upon without verification against the primary sources.
