Project 01

Macro Intelligence Brief

Official data → Deterministic calculation → AI-assisted synthesis

Completed
The Problem

Reading the macroeconomic picture means consulting several official sources by hand, retrieving each indicator separately, and comparing the latest print with its previous reading. The work is repetitive and adds no analytical value in itself.

It is also easy to do badly. Most published series are levels or indices, not rates, so comparing two adjacent observations does not produce the statistic an economist would quote. A consumer price index rising is not the same statement as inflation accelerating.

The Experiment

Delegate collection and computation entirely to tested code, then test whether a language model can turn already-validated statistics into a concise briefing.

The question is deliberately narrow. Not “can a model analyse the economy?”, but: given correct figures, can it describe them accurately without introducing numbers of its own?

Why Calculation Precedes AI

AI is not the source of economic facts.

The pipeline retrieves official data, computes and validates every statistic in tested code, and only then passes finished figures to a language model as a constrained language layer. The model never calculates, never retrieves, never selects a period and never sees unvalidated data.

The reasoning is practical. A wrong figure from a language model is a fluent wrong figure, and fluency is what makes it dangerous — the reader gets no cue. Arithmetic is cheap to make deterministic and cheap to test, so it is settled first, and the prose is then checked back against it.

Data Sources

All figures come from official statistical sources, and none is accepted without provenance.

FRED — Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis
Primary retrieval, requested as raw untransformed values.
BLS — Bureau of Labor Statistics
Independent cross-check for consumer prices.
BEA — Bureau of Economic Analysis
Independent cross-check for real GDP growth.
The Five Indicators

United States only. Each label names the transformation, not the raw series: an index or a dollar level is never itself a rate.

Each transformation is a tested function. Changes in rates are reported in percentage points, changes in levels as percentage changes.

Architecture
  1. 01

    Official Data Sources

    Statistical agencies, cited so every figure is traceable to its publisher.

  2. 02

    Collection

    Retrieval of raw values with enough history for every comparison. Live metadata is checked against the specification.

  3. 03

    Normalisation

    String values parsed, missing-value sentinels recorded rather than coerced, chronological order enforced.

  4. 04

    Economic Calculations

    The conventional statistic for each series, from tested pure functions. Periods selected by calendar date.

  5. 05

    Validation & Cross-Checks

    Metadata, history, freshness and consistency, plus comparison against the provider's own computation and the originating agencies.

  6. 06

    Validated Brief Input

    A frozen file of validated figures with units, periods, provenance and warnings. The only economic data the model may read.

  7. 07

    AI-Assisted Synthesis

    A language model drafts prose from those figures under a fixed schema. It calculates nothing.

  8. 08

    Deterministic Output Validation

    Every number, unit, period and direction claim is checked back against the input. A failing draft is rejected, never corrected.

  9. 09

    Human Review

    A person reads, corrects and approves. Required, and not automated.

Stages 01–06 and 08 are deterministic · only stage 07 involves a model

Validation Methodology

Validation runs on the data and again on the prose. Both refuse rather than repair: if the data fails, no brief input exists; if the prose fails, no brief is rendered.

On the data
Source metadata must match the declared frequency and seasonal-adjustment basis. Required calendar periods must exist. Values must be finite, dates unique and ordered.
Same-source cross-check
Our figure against the provider's own transformation of the same series, to 0.001 percentage points. A wider gap blocks publication.
Independent agency spot-check
CPI matched the BLS index exactly. Real GDP growth agreed with BEA to within its one-decimal publication rounding.
On the prose
Every number must match a supplied display value, periods must use the readable label, percent and percentage points may not be interchanged, warnings must be carried through, and forecasting, causal claims, market commentary and advice are rejected.
What Validation Caught

CPI inflation originally selected its comparison period by array position — twelve places back in the list — which is correct only if the series has no gaps. The real data is missing October 2025, so once that observation was filtered out, position twelve landed on July: thirteen months earlier, not twelve.

Nothing was malformed. No value was missing where the code looked. The arithmetic was correct; only the operands were wrong.
Period selectionComparedResult
By array positionAug 2026 vs Jul 20253.69%
By calendar dateAug 2026 vs Aug 20253.40%

The figure was wrong by a third of a percentage point and entirely plausible. No exception was raised and the tests passed, because the synthetic fixtures had no gaps. The FRED cross-check caught it.

Periods are now resolved by calendar date, and a missing required period blocks publication rather than falling back to a neighbouring observation. The lesson generalises: self-consistent code can be confidently wrong, and only an independent computation revealed it.

Limitations

United States only, five indicators. Not a macroeconomic picture.

Latest-vintage data. A past figure cannot be reproduced once the source revises it, and real GDP is revised substantially.

One validated live synthesis so far. The pipeline runs end to end; that says little yet about behaviour across datasets.

No forecasting and no investment recommendations, by design rather than by omission.

Human review is required. Every generated brief remains a draft until a person approves it.

This is a research exercise, not a production research system. There is no scheduling, monitoring or operational history.

What I Learned
Levels are not rates
Most published series are indices or levels. Choosing the transformation is an economic decision made before any code, and getting it wrong yields a number that resembles the one everyone quotes but isn't it.
Official data has gaps
October 2025 is absent from two of the five series. Real data breaks assumptions that clean fixtures cannot expose.
Plausible output can still be wrong
The mis-selected CPI figure raised no error and read perfectly well. Plausibility is what lets an error survive review.
External cross-checks earn their cost
Internal consistency proved almost nothing — the code agreed with itself while being wrong. An independent computation of the same statistic found the defect.
AI is most useful after validation, not before
Placing the model last made it useful and structurally unable to introduce a factual error. Earlier, every downstream check becomes a matter of trust.
Provenance and period semantics are research infrastructure
Reproducibility needs the retrieval date, the source vintage and the exact period behind every figure. “Latest available observation” and “latest economic period” are different things, and conflating them misleads quietly.
