# Project 03 — Geopolitical Risk Monitor

**Status: planned.** Not started. This document records the intent only.

Events → Economic transmission

## Problem

Geopolitical developments are reported continuously and at volume, but their economic relevance is
rarely stated in the reporting itself. The analytical question is not what happened, but through which
channels — energy and commodity prices, trade flows, supply chains, exchange rates, policy responses —
it plausibly transmits into economic outcomes, and with what lag.

## Intended direction

Track developments from a defined set of sources and map their plausible transmission channels into
prices, trade flows and policy.

The discipline that matters most here is **separating the observed event from the inferred
consequence**:

- The event, its date and its source are factual and must be cited.
- The transmission channel is an inference, and must be labelled as one, with its reasoning exposed.
- A language model may propose channels; it may not present an inference as an observation, and it may
  not quantify an impact.
- Human review before publication.

## Open questions

- Source selection, which is the hardest problem here — geopolitical reporting carries editorial
  slant, and source choice largely determines the output.
- How to express confidence in a proposed transmission channel honestly.
- How to avoid producing plausible-sounding narrative that is not actually supported.
- Whether any of this can be validated after the fact, and against what.

This project is deliberately last. It is the most susceptible to generating confident, well-written
analysis that happens to be unfounded, so it should only be attempted once the source-grounding and
review discipline has been proven on the two earlier projects.
