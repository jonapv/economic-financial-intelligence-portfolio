Problem and approach

The problem

Repetitive to do, and easy to do badly.

Reading the macro picture means consulting several official sources by hand and comparing each latest print with the previous one — work that adds no analytical value. And most series are levels or indices, not rates: a price index rising is not inflation accelerating.

The approach

Delegate collection and computation to tested code.

Then test whether a language model can turn already-validated statistics into a concise briefing. The question is deliberately narrow:

Given correct figures, can it describe them accurately without introducing numbers of its own?

Core principle

AI is not the source of economic facts.

01Official data is collected, transformed and validated first, in tested code.

02AI is used only after the analytical layer is complete. It never calculates, retrieves, selects a period or sees unvalidated data.

03A wrong figure from a model is a fluent wrong figure. Arithmetic is cheap to make deterministic and test, so it is settled first and the prose is checked back against it.

Architecture

Nine stages, one model call

Validation runs on the data and again on the prose. Both refuse rather than repair: if the data fails, no brief input exists; if the prose fails, no brief is rendered.

Validation methodology in detail
Data

On the data

Source metadata must match the declared frequency and seasonal-adjustment basis. Required calendar periods must exist. Values must be finite, dates unique and ordered.

Cross-check

Same-source cross-check

Our figure against the provider's own transformation of the same series, to 0.001 percentage points. A wider gap blocks publication.

Agencies

Independent agency spot-check

CPI matched the BLS index exactly. Real GDP growth agreed with BEA to within its one-decimal publication rounding.

Prose

On the prose

Every number must match a supplied display value, periods must use the readable label, percent and percentage points may not be interchanged, warnings must be carried through, and forecasting, causal claims, market commentary and advice are rejected.

Validation · Post-mortem

What validation caught

CPI inflation originally selected its comparison period by array position — twelve places back — which is correct only if the series has no gaps. Plausible did not mean correct.

Positional method Superseded

Aug 2026 vs Jul 2025

3.69%

Wrong comparison · looked plausible

Thirteen months apart, not twelve. Wrong by a third of a percentage point — and entirely plausible.

→ Caught by FRED cross-check →
Calendar method Adopted

Aug 2026 vs Aug 2025

3.40%

Correct calendar period

Periods resolved by calendar date. The arithmetic was always correct; only the operands were wrong.

  1. Missing observation

    October 2025 was absent from the official series.

  2. Wrong comparison selected

    Once filtered out, array position silently selected July as the comparison month.

  3. Output looked plausible

    No exception was raised; nothing was malformed.

  4. Synthetic tests passed

    The fixtures had no gaps, so they could not expose it.

  5. External cross-check failed

    An independent FRED computation disagreed — and caught it.

  6. Calendar-based publication rule adopted

    Required periods are resolved by calendar date; a missing one blocks publication rather than falling back to a neighbour.

Self-consistent code can be confidently wrong. Only an independent computation revealed it.

Division of labour

What the AI actually does

One constrained request from the frozen input file, returning structured JSON. Its contribution is wording and readability — real work, but not analysis, and not presented as analysis.

Scope

Limitations

Coverage

United States only

Not a complete macroeconomic picture.

Five indicators

Inflation, policy rate, growth, labour market, consumption.

Data

Latest-vintage data

A past figure cannot be reproduced once the source revises it; real GDP is revised substantially.

One validated synthesis run

The pipeline runs end to end; that says little yet about behaviour across datasets.

Purpose

No forecasting

By design rather than by omission.

No investment recommendations

Descriptive only. No interpretation of causes.

Operation

Human review required

Every generated brief remains a draft until a person approves it.

Research prototype

Not production infrastructure: no scheduling, monitoring or operational history.

Reflection

What I learned

  1. Levels are not rates.

    Choosing the transformation is an economic decision made before any code. Get it wrong and the number only resembles the one everyone quotes.

  2. Official data has gaps.

    October 2025 is absent from two of the five series. Clean fixtures cannot expose that.

  3. Plausible output can still be wrong.

    The mis-selected CPI figure raised no error and read well. Plausibility is what lets an error survive review.

  4. External cross-checks earn their cost.

    The code agreed with itself while being wrong. Only an independent computation found the defect.

  5. AI is most useful after validation, not before.

    Placed last, the model is useful and structurally unable to introduce a factual error.

  6. Provenance and period semantics are research infrastructure.

    Every figure needs its retrieval date, vintage and exact period. The latest available observation is not the latest economic period.

The full reasoning behind each lesson is in the project methodology.
