Context

Problem and approach

The problem

Repetitive to do, and easy to do badly.

Reading the macroeconomic picture means consulting several official sources by hand, retrieving each indicator separately, and comparing the latest print with its previous reading. The work adds no analytical value in itself.

Most published series are levels or indices, not rates. A consumer price index rising is not the same statement as inflation accelerating.

The approach

Delegate collection and computation to tested code.

Then test whether a language model can turn already-validated statistics into a concise briefing. The question is deliberately narrow — not “can a model analyse the economy?”, but:

Given correct figures, can it describe them accurately without introducing numbers of its own?

Core principle

AI is not the source of economic facts.

01Official data is collected, transformed and validated first, in tested code.

02AI is used only after the analytical layer is complete. It never calculates, retrieves, selects a period or sees unvalidated data.

03A wrong figure from a model is a fluent wrong figure. Arithmetic is cheap to make deterministic and test, so it is settled first and the prose is checked back against it.

Architecture

Nine stages, one model call

Validation runs on the data and again on the prose. Both refuse rather than repair: if the data fails, no brief input exists; if the prose fails, no brief is rendered.

  • Deterministic
  • AI-assisted
  • Human
  1. 01Deterministic

    Official Data Sources

    Statistical agencies, cited so every figure is traceable to its publisher.

  2. 02Deterministic

    Collection

    Raw values with enough history for every comparison. Live metadata checked against the specification.

  3. 03Deterministic

    Normalisation

    String values parsed, missing-value sentinels recorded rather than coerced, chronological order enforced.

  4. 04Deterministic

    Economic Calculations

    The conventional statistic for each series, from tested pure functions. Periods selected by calendar date.

  5. 05Deterministic

    Validation & Cross-Checks

    Metadata, history, freshness and consistency, plus comparison against the provider's own computation and the originating agencies.

  6. 06Deterministic

    Validated Brief Input

    A frozen file of validated figures with units, periods, provenance and warnings. The only economic data the model may read.

  7. 07AI-assisted

    AI-Assisted Synthesis

    A language model drafts prose from those figures under a fixed schema. It calculates nothing.

  8. 08Deterministic

    Deterministic Output Validation

    Every number, unit, period and direction claim is checked back against the input. A failing draft is rejected, never corrected.

  9. 09Human

    Human Review

    A person reads, corrects and approves. Required, and not automated.

Stages 01–06 and 08 are deterministic · only stage 07 involves a model

Validation methodology in detail
Data

On the data

Source metadata must match the declared frequency and seasonal-adjustment basis. Required calendar periods must exist. Values must be finite, dates unique and ordered.

Cross-check

Same-source cross-check

Our figure against the provider's own transformation of the same series, to 0.001 percentage points. A wider gap blocks publication.

Agencies

Independent agency spot-check

CPI matched the BLS index exactly. Real GDP growth agreed with BEA to within its one-decimal publication rounding.

Prose

On the prose

Every number must match a supplied display value, periods must use the readable label, percent and percentage points may not be interchanged, warnings must be carried through, and forecasting, causal claims, market commentary and advice are rejected.

Validation · Post-mortem

What validation caught

CPI inflation originally selected its comparison period by array position — twelve places back — which is correct only if the series has no gaps.

Positional method Superseded

Aug 2026 vs Jul 2025

3.69%

Thirteen months apart, not twelve. Wrong by a third of a percentage point — and entirely plausible.

Calendar method Adopted

Aug 2026 vs Aug 2025

3.40%

Periods resolved by calendar date. The arithmetic was always correct; only the operands were wrong.

  1. October 2025 was missing from the official series.
  2. Once filtered out, array position silently selected July as the comparison month.
  3. The output looked plausible. No exception was raised; nothing was malformed.
  4. Synthetic tests passed, because the fixtures had no gaps.
  5. The external FRED cross-check caught it: an independent computation disagreed.
  6. Required periods are now calendar-based; a missing required period blocks publication rather than falling back to a neighbour.

Self-consistent code can be confidently wrong. Only an independent computation revealed it.

Division of labour

What the AI actually does

One constrained request from the frozen input file, returning structured JSON. Its contribution is wording and readability — real work, but not analysis, and not presented as analysis.

Brief · Executive summary As of 27 September 2026

United States · Macro Intelligence Brief

United States Macroeconomic Briefing

Headline CPI inflation was broadly stable at 3.4% year-on-year in August 2026, while the unemployment rate stood unchanged at 4.1%. Retail sales increased by 1.2% month-over-month in August 2026 following −0.5% in the prior month. Real GDP expanded at an annualised quarterly rate of 1.5% in 2026 Q2, down from 2.1% in the preceding quarter. The effective federal funds rate was 3.88% on 24 September 2026, unchanged from the previous observation.

Descriptive only · no forecast · no advice

Verify every figure against the primary release before any use.

Scope

Limitations

United States only

Five indicators. Not a complete macroeconomic picture.

Five indicators

Inflation, policy rate, growth, labour market, consumption.

Latest-vintage data

A past figure cannot be reproduced once the source revises it; real GDP is revised substantially.

One validated live synthesis

The pipeline runs end to end; that says little yet about behaviour across datasets.

No forecasting

By design rather than by omission.

No investment recommendations

Descriptive only. No interpretation of causes.

Human review required

Every generated brief remains a draft until a person approves it.

Research prototype

Not production infrastructure: no scheduling, monitoring or operational history.

Reflection

What I learned

  1. Levels are not rates.

    Most published series are indices or levels. Choosing the transformation is an economic decision made before any code, and getting it wrong yields a number that resembles the one everyone quotes but isn't it.

  2. Official data has gaps.

    October 2025 is absent from two of the five series. Real data breaks assumptions that clean fixtures cannot expose.

  3. Plausible output can still be wrong.

    The mis-selected CPI figure raised no error and read perfectly well. Plausibility is what lets an error survive review.

  4. External cross-checks earn their cost.

    Internal consistency proved almost nothing — the code agreed with itself while being wrong. An independent computation of the same statistic found the defect.

  5. AI is most useful after validation, not before.

    Placing the model last made it useful and structurally unable to introduce a factual error. Earlier, every downstream check becomes a matter of trust.

  6. Provenance and period semantics are research infrastructure.

    Reproducibility needs the retrieval date, the source vintage and the exact period behind every figure. “Latest available observation” and “latest economic period” are different things, and conflating them misleads quietly.
